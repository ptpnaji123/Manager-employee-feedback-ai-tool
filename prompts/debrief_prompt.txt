THE FOUR REQUIRED CONVERSATION CHECKPOINTS
==========================================

Evaluate the manager against these four checkpoints.

A checkpoint is satisfied when the MANAGER'S INTENTION is clearly present in what the manager said.

The wording does NOT need to match the examples exactly.

Judge the meaning and intention, not the exact words.

---

## CHECKPOINT 1 — UNDERSTAND THE REASON FOR LATENESS

The manager should ask Rohit about the reason, cause, difficulty, or circumstances behind his late arrivals.

Examples of wording that satisfies this checkpoint:

* "Can you help me understand what's happening?"
* "What's been making it difficult to arrive on time?"
* "What do you think is causing the late starts?"
* "Can you tell me what's been getting in the way?"
* "Is there anything affecting your ability to get here on time?"
* "What's been happening on the mornings when you're late?"

The exact wording can be different.

The manager receives credit if the intention is clearly to understand why Rohit has been arriving late.

Simply stating that Rohit is late does NOT satisfy this checkpoint.

---

## CHECKPOINT 2 — ADDRESS THE IMPACT ON TEAM OR WORK

The manager should clearly communicate that the lateness affects the team, work, productivity, client service, coverage, communication, or another concrete work outcome.

Examples:

* "Other team members sometimes have to cover for your work."
* "When you miss the stand-up, you can miss important updates."
* "Your late arrival affects the team's ability to get started."
* "It creates additional work for the rest of the team."
* "It can delay how quickly we respond to clients."
* "I want to make sure your late arrivals aren't affecting the team's productivity."

The exact wording can be different.

The intention must be clear that the manager is explaining the WORK IMPACT of the lateness.

Simply asking:
"Do you understand?"
does NOT satisfy this checkpoint.

Simply saying:
"You're late a lot."
does NOT satisfy this checkpoint.

---

## CHECKPOINT 3 — AGREE ON BEING ON TIME GOING FORWARD

The manager should clearly ask for or establish a future commitment to arrive on time.

Examples:

* "Going forward, can we agree that you'll be here by 9:30?"
* "Can you make sure you're ready to start on time?"
* "Can we agree that this needs to improve from now on?"
* "What can we agree on to make sure you're here on time?"
* "Can you commit to starting at 9:30 going forward?"

The exact wording can be different.

The intention must clearly be about a future commitment or agreed change.

A vague statement such as:
"Things need to improve."
is not enough.

IMPORTANT:

A manager proposal alone is not automatically an agreement.

Look at Rohit's response as well.

If the manager says:
"From tomorrow, you need to be here at 9:30."

but Rohit does not respond to the proposal, this checkpoint is NOT fully satisfied.

If Rohit clearly accepts the proposed change, then it is satisfied.

---

## CHECKPOINT 4 — FOLLOW-UP IN TWO WEEKS

The manager should establish a follow-up to review progress.

The expected intention is a check-in approximately two weeks later.

Examples:

* "Let's check in again in two weeks."
* "We'll review this in two weeks and see how things are going."
* "Let's meet again after two weeks to see how you're doing."
* "I'd like us to follow up in two weeks."
* "Let's revisit this in two weeks."

The exact wording can be different.

The intention must clearly be a future follow-up/check-in.

Simply saying:
"We'll see how it goes."
does NOT satisfy this checkpoint unless a future follow-up is clearly established.

IMPORTANT — DO NOT REQUIRE EXACT QUESTIONS

The four checkpoints are based on INTENTION, not exact wording.

For example:

Likewise:

"How do you think your late arrivals affect the team?"

and:

"When you're late, other team members sometimes have to cover your work."

can both satisfy Checkpoint 2.

Do NOT use TALK TO HR merely because:

* the manager missed checkpoints
* the conversation was too short

* the manager did not ask enough questions
